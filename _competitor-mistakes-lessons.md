## 2. The FTC earnings-claim graveyard — honesty is a legal MOAT, not a constraint
**Public record:**
- **FTC standard:** it is a deceptive practice to misrepresent that participants "will or are likely to earn any specific amount or percentage." Any earnings/win claim needs written substantiation that it's **TYPICAL** for users.
- **RagingBull.com — $2.425M FTC settlement** (direct analog: an online STOCK-PICKS subscription service): used **bogus earnings claims** to drive signups → paid $2.4M, BARRED from earnings claims "without written evidence those claims are typical for consumers," AND dinged for **hard-to-cancel subscriptions** (ROSCA / negative-option).
- **DraftKings/FanDuel (2016 DFS false-advertising):** ads implied all players had equal chances of winning; used **pro-player testimonials as if typical** without disclosure.
**LESSON:** a subscription service that sells on unsubstantiated win/earnings claims is a textbook FTC target ($2.4M + injunction). Fake "95% winners" tout claims (scores24-style) are illegal, not just sleazy.
**GSE weaponization (turn compliance into offense):**
- GSE can LEGALLY claim what competitors can't — because it substantiates: publish ACTUAL out-of-sample CLV vs Pinnacle + realized win-rate WITH the real sample size and dates; never a promise, always a receipt.
- The market splits into two legally-unsafe camps GSE avoids: touts making illegal fake claims (FTC risk) and incumbents making NO accountability claim (empty flank). GSE = the only legally-safe accountable position.
- Guardrails baked in: (a) never display a number that isn't a substantiated realized result; (b) never imply typical-user results = best-case; (c) any testimonial must be representative + disclosed; (d) frictionless cancel (FTC click-to-cancel). These are ALREADY GSE doctrine ([[project-gse-proprietary-rating-mandate]], humanizer/draft-only) — now with the case law behind why.
→ Aggressive framing: "Everyone claims 90%. We publish our actual record — hits AND misses — measured against the sharpest line in the world. Nobody else will show you that, because most of them legally can't."

## 3. The pick'em war — player-vs-house prop pick'em is illegal betting; free/skill or P2P is the safe harbor
**Public record:** PrizePicks **ordered to pay $15M + cease in NY**; Underdog **$17.5M NY** settlement; cease-and-desists in **10+ states** (NY, MI, FL, MD, MA, OH, CT, IA, TN, IL). FL Gaming Control Commission: pick'em = "illegal bets or wagers" AND "an illegal lottery"; skill-contest betting "strictly prohibited." Both operators **restructured to PEER-TO-PEER** (PrizePicks "Arena," Underdog P2P) — "outcomes determined by players' decisions, not by odds from the operators."
**LESSON:** the illegal element = **player-vs-HOUSE, on player props, for money** (looks like a sportsbook/lottery). The legal restructure = P2P player-vs-player skill.
**GSE weaponization:** GSE's "Beat the Model" must be **FREE (no entry fee, no prize pool = no bet/wager/lottery)** — that avoids the ENTIRE war outright ($15M+$17.5M just in NY proves the cost of getting it wrong). If GSE ever monetizes a contest, it MUST be P2P/skill, never house-vs-player-on-props. Free-skill isn't "playing it safe" — it's the design that lets GSE operate in all 50 states while PrizePicks/Underdog fight state-by-state. Attack framing: "We'll never take your money to bet against you."

## 4. The Kalshi jurisdictional battle — protect GSE's CLV-proof source
**Public record:** **Third Circuit (Apr 6, 2026): CFTC has EXCLUSIVE jurisdiction** over sports-related event contracts; Kalshi likely to prevail that they're "swaps" under the Commodity Exchange Act and the CEA **preempts state gambling law** (first fed appeals ruling, pro-Kalshi).
