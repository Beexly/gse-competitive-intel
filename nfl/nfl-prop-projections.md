### Passing Yards

Sportsbooks will offer a passing yardage total for each starting quarterback in the game. These are adjusted based on the player's ability, the matchup, and the potential game script. Let's review an example of [Kansas City Chiefs](https://www.actionnetwork.com/nfl/odds/kansas-city-chiefs) quarterback Patrick Mahomes.

- Patrick Mahomes: 254.5 (over -112, under -108)

If you bet Mahomes over 254.5, he would need at least 255 passing yards for you to win your wager. You would need to risk $1.12 for every dollar you want to win. This prop works similarly to rushing and receiving yard props.

### Anytime Touchdown

Some sportsbooks may give you two options (also known as a two-way market) on whether a player will score a touchdown or not. Users can bet either "Yes,” as in you're betting on them to score a touchdown, or "No.”

Let's use an example from FanDuel for a [Cardinals](https://www.actionnetwork.com/nfl/odds/arizona-cardinals) vs. [Rams](https://www.actionnetwork.com/nfl/odds/los-angeles-rams) game:

- Puka Nacua to Score Anytime: +150 ($10 risk wins $15)

Anytime touchdowns cover rushing, receiving, or special teams touchdowns! They do not cover passing touchdowns as these are a completely separate market. At a sportsbook like ESPN BET, they offer odds on the "Yes" and "No" for anytime touchdown scorers.
