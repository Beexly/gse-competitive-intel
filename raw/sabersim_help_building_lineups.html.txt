These simulations account for score, clock, matchups, coaching decisions, and game flow to produce realistic game scripts.</p></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p> </p></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p>From there, SaberSim builds lineups that reflect those stories:</p></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-unordered-nested-list"><ul><li><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p><b>Stacks and correlations emerge naturally</b> when players succeed together in the same simulation.</p></div></li><li><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p><b>Players are judged across their full range of outcomes</b>, not just an average.</p></div></li><li><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p><b>Upside is built in</b> without needing complicated rules or exposure caps.</p></div></li></ul></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p>The result is a pool of lineups that reflect how slates actually unfold.</p></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p> </p></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p>This reflects a core truth of DFS: you aren’t betting on a spreadsheet—you’re betting on real, messy games. In SaberSim, when a QB and WR succeed in the same script, they appear together in lineups because that’s how the story actually played out. No guesswork, no fragile rules...just reality modeled directly into your builds.</p></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-horizontal-rule"><hr/></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-subheading intercom-interblocks-align-left"><h2 id="h_f01da3d1ae"><b>What’s the recommended lineup building process?</b></h2></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p>The best approach is: <b>Build → Review → Revise.</b></p></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-ordered-nested-list"><ol><li><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p><b>Build</b>: Generate a large pool of lineups (as many as your plan allows) using default Sim Builder settings.</p></div></li><li><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p><b>Review</b>: Check exposures by player, position, team, game, and stacks.</p></div></li><li><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p><b>Revise</b>: Make targeted adjustments, such as projection tweaks, min/max exposures, or filters.</p></div></li></ol></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p>Avoid starting with heavy rules. Let the sims build first, then refine.</p></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p> </p></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p>Your first build doesn&#x27;t have to be your final product. It’s a starting point. The sims surface realistic game stories, and your role is to make small adjustments that reflect your research and contest strategy. </p></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-horizontal-rule"><hr/></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p> </p></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-subheading intercom-interblocks-align-left"><h2 id="h_889a2f3bbf"><b>Why should I build a large lineup pool if I’m only playing a few?</b></h2></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-unordered-nested-list"><ul><li><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p>Your highest ROI lineups aren’t built in order. SaberSim samples outcomes randomly, so your “best” lineup might be the 200th one built.</p></div></li><li><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p>Larger pools give the Portfolio Diversifier more flexibility to assemble an optimal portfolio.</p></div></li><li><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p>A big pool lets you explore the slate more fully:</p></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-unordered-nested-list"><ul><li><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p>See which stacks and environments SaberSim favors.</p></div></li><li><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p>Identify leverage spots.</p></div></li><li><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p>Refine your exposures more effectively.</p></div></li></ul></div></li></ul></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p>Even if you only plan to enter a handful of lineups, building a large pool first ensures you’re selecting from the best possible mix of realistic game stories.</p></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-horizontal-rule"><hr/></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p> </p></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-subheading intercom-interblocks-align-left"><h2 id="h_74daeda38e"><b>What’s the difference between adjusting projections and setting exposures?</b></h2></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-unordered-nested-list"><ul><li><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p><b>Adjusting projections</b>: Changes how a player performs in the sims, which indirectly affects how often they appear in your lineups. Use this when you think projections are off but you don’t know exactly how much exposure you want to a player. It keeps the process “honest” by letting the sims still decide what’s optimal.</p></div></li><li><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p><b>Setting exposures</b>: Directly forces a player into your lineups at a set percentage. Use this when you know for sure that you want a certain amount of a player in your lineups regardless of how they perform in the sims.</p></div></li></ul></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p>Projections guide the <i>stories</i> you want to bet on, exposures guide the <i>portfolio</i> of lineups you want to play. Use projections to shape the game-level narrative, and exposures to manage your personal risk and contest mix.</p></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-horizontal-rule"><hr/></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p> </p></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-subheading intercom-interblocks-align-left"><h2 id="h_02229460ea"><b>What’s the difference between Sim Mode and Optimizer Mode?</b></h2></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-unordered-nested-list"><ul><li><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p><b>Sim Mode</b>: Builds from simulated game outcomes. Stacks, leverage, and correlation happen naturally because players excelled together in specific scripts.</p></div></li><li><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p><b>Optimizer Mode</b>: Uses static projections only. Useful for cash games or testing projections, but less effective for GPPs.</p></div></li></ul></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p>Sim Mode is designed for playing to win in GPPs. Optimizer Mode is better for flat-payout contests where floor matters more than ceiling.</p></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-horizontal-rule"><hr/></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p> </p></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-subheading intercom-interblocks-align-left"><h2 id="h_0117b53c01"><b>What do the Sim Diversity and Correlation sliders do?</b></h2></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-unordered-nested-list"><ul><li><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p><b>Sim Diversity</b>: Controls how many different simulations are used. Higher = more variety across game outcomes. Lower = tighter builds around fewer outcomes.</p></div></li><li><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p><b>Correlation</b>: Controls how strongly correlated plays are favored. Higher = more stacking. Lower = more one-offs.</p></div></li></ul></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p>These sliders are your way to fine-tune which truths you want to emphasize. Higher Sim Diversity embraces the chaos of sports. Higher Correlation doubles down on game-level storytelling.</p></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-horizontal-rule"><hr/></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p> </p></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-subheading intercom-interblocks-align-left"><h2 id="h_07acee5545"><b>Why do my lineups look different every time I build, even without changes?</b></h2></div><div class="intercom-interblocks-paragraph no-margin intercom-interblocks-align-left"><p>Each build samples a new set of simulations. Since no two slates play out identically, no two builds should look the same.
